# Research the craft before making the thing

Driving the tool is not the skill. Knowing what a good one looks like is.

Example of the failure: asked for a product demo video, an agent recorded
itself clicking through every tab. Every part worked and the video was useless,
because the first rule of demo videos is to show one to three things, never
everything. Nobody checked what makes a demo good.

## The rule

When the thing you are making has people who make it for a living (landing
pages, sign-up forms, emails, slide decks, onboarding flows, announcement
posts, logos, READMEs), spend the first few minutes learning the genre:

1. **Find three good examples and one teardown.** A teardown by a professional
   explains what they were deciding, not just what they chose.
2. **Pull out rules, not vibes.** "Ask for 3 fields, not 12" is a rule. "Make
   it engaging" is nothing.
3. **Write the rules down** in the project's `CLAUDE.md` or a skill, so the
   next session starts with them.
4. **Build once those three are done.**

## When the request contradicts the craft

The person asking usually is not an expert in the genre either. If the request
names a known mistake ("put every ministry on the homepage"), say which rule it
breaks in one line, build the version that works, and offer the literal version
too if they still want it.

## Check the premise

When an idea rests on a claim about the world ("nobody makes a check-in app for
small churches"), search it before building on it. Say what exists, with links,
and where the real gap is.

## Never one-shot creative work

For copy, a page design, a script or a post:

1. Find the model (examples you admire, or the user's own best writing).
2. Write down what makes it work: structure, length, tone.
3. Draft one section at a time against that model.
4. Compare the whole draft side by side with the model before calling it done.

## The test

Before producing, answer: what do people who do this for a living say separates
a good one from a bad one? No answer means the research step got skipped.
